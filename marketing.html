Gears of Silver

The workshop smelled of oil and cold brass. Anežka lit the lamp and bent over the clock her father had never finished, its hundred tiny wheels waiting in their velvet tray.

Somewhere beneath them, the old town bells began to count the hour — one, two, three — and on the thirteenth stroke, the silver gears began to turn on their own.

She did not breathe. The hands swept backwards once, twice, and stopped at a time that did not exist on any clock in Prague.

"So it was true," she whispered.
